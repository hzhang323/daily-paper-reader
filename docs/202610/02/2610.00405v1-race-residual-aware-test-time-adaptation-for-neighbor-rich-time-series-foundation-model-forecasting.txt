# RACE: Residual-Aware Test-Time Adaptation for Neighbor-Rich Time-Series Foundation Model Forecasting 

Hao-Nan Shi ∗ Tong Wu ∗ Chen-Cong Sun Yuan Jiang Han-Jia Ye De-Chuan Zhan †

School of Artificial Intelligence, Nanjing University National Key Laboratory for Novel Software Technology, Nanjing University Nanjing, China 

Abstract 

Time-series foundation models (TSFMs) perform strongly across forecasting tasks, but their per-series inference is ill-suited to neighbor-rich forecasting , where each query has access to related but nonidentical historical series. Contin-uous glucose monitoring (CGM) and Web/cloud workloads exemplify this setting: CGM trajectories share physiologi-cal patterns but vary across individuals, devices, and con-ditions, while Web/cloud workloads combine common oper-ating regimes with non-stationarity, heavy tails, and bursts. Such histories contain useful shared structure, yet individual neighbors are not equally relevant. Existing methods either fine-tune TSFMs for each target domain, incurring additional costs and offering limited transferability across backbones, or append retrieved series without verifying whether they sup-port the current forecast. The key challenges are conflicting residual evidence from neighboring series and residual pat-terns that vary across TSFMs and forecasting tasks. We for-mulate test-time neighborhood scaling: we directly leverage same-domain neighbor evidence without modifying the back-bone. We propose RACE (Residual-Aware Correction of Fore-casting Errors), a two-stage framework for selecting and uti-lizing historical neighbors. We first retrieve query-compatible neighbors, align their residuals to the query scale, and aggre-gate coherent evidence into the training-free RACE-TF cor-rection. Full RACE then uses a lightweight, domain-specific Gate to determine when applying the correction is beneficial, with a training workflow that can be reused across TSFM backbones. Across four TSFMs, RACE improves all three domain-aggregate metrics on both primary domains, with the largest gains on high-error queries. Within each domain, a Gate trained on one TSFM transfers to other backbones without adaptation, and the resulting pipeline improves all 72 cross-backbone metric comparisons over the matched frozen targets.

These approaches motivate a more specific question: do similar histories produce residuals that support the same correction? Neighbor residuals may con-flict, and their patterns vary across TSFMs and forecasting tasks. Direct averaging can therefore degrade the forecast, while a backbone-specific fusion interface can complicate reuse across models. We formulate this problem as test-time neighborhood scal-ing : using a domain library as a neighborhood of tempo-rally valid, same-domain evidence to improve a query fore-cast without updating the TSFM backbone. We propose RACE (Residual-Aware Correction of Forecasting Errors), a two-stage framework that separates evidence construction from correction selection. Residual Evidence Construction retrieves query-compatible neighbors using both the query context and the frozen forecast, aligns their residuals to the query scale, and aggregates consistent evidence. The resulting consensus-mean proposal defines the RACE-TF variant, where TF denotes training-free. Full RACE adds a lightweight domain-specific Gate that uses inference-visible information to decide whether to apply the correction. The Gate is fitted on development data and frozen at deploy-ment; test-time adaptation here changes the forecast output, not the backbone parameters. The same fitting procedure is used across TSFMs, and the transfer experiments addition-ally reuse a fitted source Gate and its threshold. We evaluate RACE using four TSFMs in two application domains, CGM and Web/cloud, each drawing on multiple datasets. For reproducibility, CGM performance is evaluated on the public dataset, with the private collections used only as retrieval resources. Our analyses cover forecasting accu-racy, mechanism behavior, efficiency, and cross-backbone transfer. RACE improves all three domain-aggregate metrics in both primary domains across the four backbones, with the largest gains on queries for which the frozen backbone has high prediction error. Further analyses show that residual ev-idence construction remains effective without target-domain training and selective application improves the reliability of candidate corrections. A source-trained Gate can also be reused on other TSFMs within the same domain without target-specific fitting or recalibration: all 72 cross-backbone metric comparisons improve on the matched frozen targets, with average gains close to those of target-trained RACE. Our contributions are threefold. We define neighbor-rich forecasting and formulate the use of a reusable domain library as test-time neighborhood scaling . We introduce RACE, which constructs a consensus residual correction and selectively applies it; its evidence-construction stage also yields the training-free RACE-TF variant. Finally, exper-iments in the CGM and Web/cloud domains across four TSFMs show domain-aggregate forecasting gains, partic-ularly on high-error queries, and demonstrate that trained Gates can be reused across backbones without target-specific fitting or recalibration. 

## Related Work 

Time-series foundation models and deployment resid-uals. Time-series foundation models extend zero-shot forecasting through broad pretraining, with probabilistic language-style modeling in Chronos (Ansari et al., 2024a), 2decoder-only modeling in TimesFM (Das et al., 2024), uni-versal training in Moirai (Woo et al., 2024), and more re-cent universal or scaling-oriented families in Chronos-2 and Toto-2.0 (Ansari et al., 2025; Khwaja et al., 2026). GIFT-Eval, a benchmark for general time-series forecasting, makes cross-domain, leakage-aware evaluation explicit (Aksu et al., 2024). RACE is not another backbone. It studies a post-deployment question left open by a frozen backbone: whether its own legally observed residual history can refine a later output under a disciplined information boundary. 

Retrieval and post-hoc forecasting correction. Retrieval-based forecasting uses related historical sequences as refer-ences (Jing et al., 2022). Retrieval Augmented Forecasting (RAF) retrieves examples for training-free input augmenta-tion (Tire et al., 2026). TimeRAG uses dynamic time warp-ing (DTW) to retrieve sequences for a large language model (LLM) prompt (Yang et al., 2025). TimeRAF is a retrieval-augmented foundation model with learned retrieval and in-tegration (Zhang et al., 2025); TS-RAG applies retrieval-augmented generation to time series through an adaptive fusion module with a frozen backbone (Ning et al., 2025). Retrieval Augmented Time Series Forecasting (RAFT) in-stead learns to combine retrieved contexts and futures during forecasting-model training (Han et al., 2025). Context-based calibration adapts a prediction head using context-similar samples (Chen et al., 2024). These methods operate on se-quence content or learned predictors; RACE aligns and filters the frozen forecaster’s own historical residuals before decid-ing whether to modify its output. Optimization-Free Test-Time Adaptation (OFTTA) adapts normalization and support-set prototypes for wearable activ-ity recognition without backpropagation (Wang et al., 2023). Such adaptation is distinct from RACE’s output-side resid-ual correction. In full RACE, the Gate is fitted before deploy-ment; only the evidence-construction variant is training-free. 

Case-based prediction and personalization in health. 

Case-based glucose prediction estimates future glucose from similar historical trajectories and studies how distance mea-sures, neighbor counts, and aggregation affect accuracy (Zulj et al., 2021). Neighbor Fusion Personalization combines a pretrained population predictor with population and personal neighbor estimates, adjusting their contribution as personal history accumulates without retraining (Indriani et al., 2026). GlyRAG combines glucose-derived textual context and re-trieved episodes through learned retrieval adapters and fu-sion; its LLM-generated descriptions can be cached offline (Soumma and Ghasemzadeh, 2026). For wearable stress de-tection, frozen foundation-model embeddings support re-trieval from a user’s history and personalization of down-stream representations (Simon and Chetouani, 2026). These studies establish the value of historical cases and lightweight personalization. RACE addresses a specific correction ob-ject: residuals produced by the same frozen forecaster on completed windows. It aligns and filters these residuals into a consensus proposal, then uses a utility-trained Gate to apply the proposal or retain the base output. Our experiments eval-uate this residual-correction mechanism rather than claim a performance comparison with these health-specific systems. 

## Problem Definition and Challenges 

3.1 From Time-Series to Neighbor-Rich Forecasting 

A standard forecasting task maps a query window’s observed context to its unobserved future. Under the per-series in-ference protocol considered here, a frozen TSFM does so without consulting completed records in the deployment en-vironment. Here, we consider neighbor-rich forecasting , in which a target query is accompanied by a library of related but non-identical series. Completed windows provide legiti-mately available realized futures, frozen-model forecasts, and residuals; these outcome-observed historical windows are reusable evidence. Continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) and Web/cloud workload forecasting are representative set-tings. CGM trajectories share physiological dynamics but differ by individual, device, and state; workload series share operating regimes but exhibit non-stationarity, bursts, and heavy tails. Their libraries contain useful common structure, but no individual neighbor is directly interchangeable with the target. While this is our primary setting, Appendix E.1 reports 34 GIFT-Eval subsets meeting the fixed-window re-quirements without a predefined narrow neighbor relation, where the same legal retrieval and residual-evidence process can still identify reusable windows. 

3.2 In-Domain Residual Adaptation 

A frozen TSFM learns broad temporal regularities from large-scale, multi-domain pretraining, yet per-series infer-ence without retrieval ignores both completed related win-dows in the deployment domain and its realized errors on them. This domain library is already available and requires no backbone update. This enables output-side correction: when a completed window and a query are comparable in temporal and frozen-model forecasting behavior, the former’s residual can inform the latter’s correction. The TSFM remains frozen, and only earlier outcome-observed domain records modify its output.

3.4 Challenges and Motivation 

A large domain library provides many candidate windows, but only some carry errors that are relevant to the current query. Similar observed trajectories can lead to different fu-tures, and the same trajectory may expose different weak-nesses in different frozen TSFMs. Retrieval must therefore account for both the visible context and the model’s forecast, while excluding outcomes that were unavailable at prediction time. Even among compatible neighbors, residuals can differ in scale or point in conflicting directions. A useful correction must align these records and identify a shared pattern rather than average every retrieved error. Agreement alone, how-ever, does not guarantee that the correction will help an indi-vidual query. Together, these observations define three inter-related challenges: (i) effective neighbors are scarce within a large reusable library; (ii) retrieved information must be converted into a robust correction proposal; and (iii) even such a proposal can harm the forecast, so its reliability must be judged. 

## RACE Method 

As shown in Figure 2, RACE draws on a reusable domain library and has two stages for selecting and using historical neighbor evidence. Residual Evidence Construction selects query-compatible neighbors, removes conflicting residuals, and aggregates the remainder into a training-free consensus correction; this stage alone is RACE-TF. Since a proposal can still be beneficial, neutral, or harmful, the Utility-Aware Correction Gate learns when to use it. 

4.1 Residual Evidence Construction 

Residual evidence is informative only when a completed window resembles the query in both its observed trajectory and frozen-model forecast, because the residual reflects both. We therefore retrieve neighbors using a joint context–forecast descriptor rather than history alone. Our default raw-z representation concatenates the query context and frozen forecast, both z-normalized using query-history statistics. Memory records retain the corresponding descriptors, residuals, scales, and provenance. RACE re-trieves K = 60 eligible neighbors by squared Euclidean distance without accessing the query future, then aligns their residuals to the query scale using a clipped ratio. Residual consensus filters conflicting evidence before aggregation. Our mean-centred residual-consensus filter uses median absolute deviation (MAD) for normalization and removes neighbors whose query-scale-aligned residuals are displaced from, or more variable than, the retrieved set. It forms a coordinate-wise mean centre over usable coordinates. ## Experimental Setup 

We evaluate RACE in two neighbor-rich domains under a common chronological protocol, using four frozen back-bones and matched forecasting comparisons. 

5.1 Data and Temporal Protocol 

Web comprises six GIFT-Eval subsets (Aksu et al., 2024) and four Web/cloud workload subsets from the Large-scale Open Time Series Archive (LOTSA) (Woo et al., 2024). CGM uses the public ShanghaiT2DM collection (Zhao et al., 2023) for target evaluation and four de-identified institutional collections solely as retrieval memory. Their completed win-dows supply historical residual evidence, but no Gate-fitting, model-selection, calibration, or test labels. The public CGM target collection is never an evidence source. Every experiment uses a 512-step context and a 64-step forecast horizon. Future-label boundaries divide target se-ries into fit (0–70%), model-select (70–80%), calibration (80–90%), and test (90–100%) sets. For Web, the union of fit windows from the ten logical subsets forms the resid-ual pool; cross-series candidates are permitted, subject to the same-parent temporal rule and self-exclusion. CGM re-trieves only from its four external collections. All test futures and residuals are excluded from retrieval, feature construc-tion, threshold selection, and model selection. Appendices B and C document source manifests, window sampling, over-lap checks, privacy and release boundaries, and the leakage audit.

Each backbone has independent caches of forecasts, residuals, representations, candidates, propos-als, and Gate features; residuals are never shared across models. ## Results 

The experiments address five connected questions: (1) whether RACE improves end-to-end forecasting across frozen backbones and both domains; (2) whether query-conditioned retrieval and residual consensus improve residual-evidence quality; (3) whether the Utility-Aware Cor-rection Gate enriches beneficial corrections while retaining useful coverage; (4) whether the gains can be explained by global bias or a history-only query; and (5) whether a Gate trained on one TSFM can retain useful corrections on other frozen backbones without target-specific fitting or recalibra-tion.

6.2 Cross-Backbone Gate Transfer 

Within each domain, we transfer each source-trained Gate, with its feature schema and threshold fixed, to the other three TSFMs. Targets retain their own forecasts, residual pools, and consensus proposals, without Gate refitting, model selection, or recalibration. Two domains, four sources, three distinct targets, and three metrics yield 2 × 4 × 3 × 3 = 72 off-diagonal comparisons. Table 2 summarizes the results. Gate uplift averages the increment over target RACE-TF across three metrics and the three other targets within each domain, in percentage points. The Gate adds considerably more on Web/cloud, consis-tently improving MAE and CRPS across source–target pairs. This is consistent with filtering harmful corrections in a vari-able, burst-prone setting, despite an MSE trade-off relative to RACE-TF. On CGM, RACE-TF already provides strong gains across backbones and metrics, leaving smaller, occa-sionally negative Gate increments. These results suggest that selection matters most when neighborhood evidence is less reliable. Source-trained Gates nevertheless preserve useful corrections across backbones without target-specific fitting or recalibration; Appendix E.2 provides the metric-wise re-sults.

6.6 Qualitative Case Analysis 

Figure 5 examines individual Chronos-2 forecasts using RACE, RAF, and Residual Mean, the ablation without con-sensus or the Gate. In the upper CGM case, the retrieved neighborhood supports a future glucose rise that the frozen forecast largely misses. Both RACE and Residual Mean re-cover part of this rise, with RACE giving a slightly larger improvement; RAF makes only a small adjustment and re-mains close to the underestimated baseline. In the lower Web case, the retrieved history follows the query’s broad shape, but rapid fluctuations dominate the observed future. The smoother Chronos-2 forecast already tracks its local cen-tral level reasonably well, leaving little scope for a smooth residual adjustment; all three corrections instead worsen the forecast. This behavior highlights a difficulty also encoun-tered when similar Web histories lead to different continua-tions: historical resemblance alone does not ensure transfer-able future or residual structure. Strong irregular fluctuations can further limit the benefit of either input augmentation or output correction. These observations suggest that the use-fulness of a neighbor-rich library depends on the underlying data distribution. Reliable patterns shared across series, to-gether with relatively weak irregular high-frequency varia-tion, make the smoother CGM setting particularly favorable for neighbor-based enhancement. 

6.7 Robustness 

On the broader diagnostic over 34 eligible GIFT-Eval sub-sets, RACE improves 11 of the 12 backbone–metric ag-gregates; Chronos-2 CRPS increases slightly, by 0.089%. In cross-backbone transfer, the complete pipeline preserves most of the gains of target-trained correction and improves over matched frozen targets in 72 /72 domain–source–target– metric comparisons (Section 6.2). Encoder, neighborhood-size, retrieval-scope, and Gate-target diagnostics show where the gains persist and where they weaken (Appendix E). In par-ticular, domain-level improvements do not imply improve-ments for every Web subset: Appendix E.4 reports the cor-responding win/loss counts. 

## Conclusion 

RACE refines frozen-TSFM forecasts by aggregating com-patible residuals from completed same-domain windows and selectively applying the correction through a lightweight utility gate. Across four TSFMs and two neighbor-rich do-mains, it delivers consistent domain-aggregate gains through lightweight post-processing, establishing historical model er-rors as reusable test-time evidence. 9References

## B Data Scope, Domain Libraries, and Release Boundary 

The following task manifest fixes the data sources and retrieval boundary. The ten Web subsets are six public GIFT-Eval Web/cloud workload subsets (two Bitbrains (Shen et al., 2015) and four BizitObs (Saha et al., 2024) logical subsets) and four public LOTSA subsets: Alibaba Cluster Trace 2018 (Alibaba Group, 2018), Azure virtual-machine traces from 2017 (Cortez et al., 2017), Borg Cluster Data 2011 (Reiss et al., 2011), and Extended Web Traffic with Missing Values (Godahewa et al., 2022). The processed Web manifest contains 15,541 univariate series; their fit windows form the Web domain library, from which each query’s eligible residual pool is derived, so no test row is evidence. The CGM task uses 109 public ShanghaiT2DM records as targets and four de-identified collections supplied by collaborating data holders as a retrieval-only domain library; Shang-haiT2DM is never evidence. The broader GIFT-Eval diagnostic evaluates 34 subsets satisfying the fixed-window requirements, with each query’s eligible residual pool derived from fit windows; it is separate from the primary Web setting. No raw CGM record, identifier map, physical unit, or source collection name is part of the prepared reproducibility package. Instead, the release-only CGM fixture contains 498 series under release-only identifiers (109 ShanghaiT2DM targets and 389 retrieval-only series). It removes original and parent identifiers, absolute times, units, source names, raw locations, scales, 13 and reversible mappings. Each retained series is independently robust-normalized, clipped to [−8, 8] , and quantized to three decimals. Thus, the fixture supports functional verification without reproducing the original clinical records or their reported values. To keep the prepared reproducibility package practical to distribute and run, this fixture uses a fixed 64-step replay grid obtained by subsampling the production stride-32 cache; it also releases frozen point forecasts rather than full predictive distributions. Candidate order and invalid-slot semantics are preserved, but the replay contains fewer windows and uses transformed values. Its rerun metrics can therefore show small numerical differences from the main-paper results, and its point-forecast CRPS is not a replacement for the reported distributional CRPS. The frozen stride-32 ledgers remain the source of the reported results. The public-data setup guide identifies the official acquisition sources and the required local directory contract without distributing source data. All experiments use a 512-step observed context and a 64-step forecast horizon. A model-specific cache stores frozen forecasts, residuals, representations, candidate lists, correction proposals, and Gate features. The cache is independent for every backbone; residuals are never exchanged across frozen models. All source records are expanded to univariate series before windowing. A valid cache row contains 512 observed context steps followed by a 64-step forecast horizon, and consecutive candidate starts are separated by 32 steps. Temporal roles are assigned from the future-label boundary before any capacity limit is applied; hence a cap never moves a window from one role into another. For the primary Web task, the fit, model-select, and calibration windows together have a deterministic cap of 4,000 windows per logical subset and 400 per original univariate series. The test side has the same separate limits. The front-90% allocation is newest-first round-robin within the 70:10:10 role quotas, whereas the test allocation begins with the earliest valid windows after the 90% boundary. This keeps long series from dominating a logical subset while preserving the chronological role contract. The broader GIFT-Eval diagnostic uses the same 512/64 window and stride-32 construction, with separate front-90% and test caps of 2,000 windows per logical subset and 200 per original univariate series. A prepared logical subset enters this diagnostic only when at least one univariate series yields a valid 512+64 window after the observed-mask checks and role construction. Consequently, 34 of the 55 official logical subsets are represented; the remaining 21 are recorded as having no usable window, principally because their series are too short for this window specification. No context or forecast horizon is shortened to admit those subsets. 

## C Temporal Protocol and Leakage Audit 

Target series are divided by future-label boundaries into fit (0–70%), model-select (70–80%), calibration (80–90%), and test (90–100%) roles. Fit supplies correction and Gate labels; model-select chooses correction checkpoints and tree count; calibration freezes the Gate threshold; test is evaluated only after all such choices are frozen. A window that crosses a boundary may be retrieval-only but never supplies supervision or evaluation for another role. Web uses the fixed union of the ten logical subsets’ fit windows as its domain library; each query’s eligible residual pool is derived from that library. Cross-series candidates are allowed, but a candidate from the same parent must finish no later than the query future starts, and a query cannot retrieve itself. CGM uses four de-identified external collections as a retrieval-only domain library. The public ShanghaiT2DM target collection is never an evidence source. The reproducibility materials include parent and source manifests, candidate shortfall and exact-expansion records, and window-level overlap audits. Candidate construction never reads a query future, residual, utility, or test label. A test loader is constructed only after model-selection and threshold decisions are frozen.

E.3.1 Direct learned residual-regression control. The Consensus-Evidence Residual Regressor (CERR) is a matched diag-nostic control for separating the value of a learned residual mapping from the value of utility-aware selection. ## G Utility-Aware Correction Gate Contract 

This appendix fixes the input, output, and fitting contract of the Utility-Aware Correction Gate. The ordered feature schema is serialized and hashed with each fitted model; inference fails if feature membership or order differs from the fitted schema. I.2 Reproducibility Package and Material Availability 

The separately prepared reproducibility package contains fixed configurations, offline environment and integrity checks, a compact result ledger, unit tests, a small public-data-derived Web fixture, and an irreversible CGM functional fixture. These fixtures exercise the complete post-forecast route—legal retrieval, residual evidence construction, Utility-Aware Correction Gate inference, and metric aggregation—while the ledger verifier recomputes the reported aggregate claims from its underlying de-identified records. As detailed in Appendix B, the CGM fixture is a capacity-bounded stride-64, transformed-data replay; small deviations from the frozen main-paper metrics are expected and do not replace the frozen stride-32 ledgers. The fixtures are functional verification materials; they are not a replacement for the full benchmark data or an independent reproduction of every reported paper number. The reproducibility package described here is separate from the manuscript source archive. It has been prepared for functional verification, but is not bundled with this preprint, and this version provides no public download link. It does not contain full Web data, model weights, checkpoints, raw clinical records, identifier mappings, or third-party source code. Public redistribution of the fixtures requires the applicable permissions and release terms. For a full Web run, users may invoke the documented public-asset setup utility explicitly, accepting the relevant source licenses, or supply the required data and checkpoints locally. Experiment runners do not download missing assets implicitly or silently substitute alternatives. Release of the private CGM retrieval collections remains subject to the applicable approvals and redistribution process. 

## J Limitations 

RACE is designed for neighbor-rich forecasting, where a legal residual pool contains repeated or related trajectories that can provide query-specific residual evidence. Its strongest improvements therefore occur in such settings. The broader GIFT-Eval analysis shows that the procedure can remain useful when domains are more heterogeneous and neighborhood structure is less explicit, but it does not establish uniformly large gains across general forecasting collections. The results should consequently be interpreted as evidence for residual-evidence retrieval in neighbor-rich deployments, not as a guarantee of the same improvement magnitude whenever a retrieval pool is available. The architecture is intentionally low-capacity: residual consensus is parameter-free and the learned component is a small output-side Gate. This design isolates and tests the value of legally retrieved neighbor information without adapting a TSFM backbone or relying on a backbone-specific hidden-state interface. It is not a complete study of high-capacity retrievers or correction modules, nor of large-scale retrieval libraries and broadly distributed test suites. Extending the design to larger and 24 more diverse memory collections, evaluating richer learned residual-transfer models, and characterizing their scaling behavior are important directions for future work. The clinical-data experiments are retrospective forecasting analyses, not validation for diagnosis, treatment recommendations, or insulin dosing.
