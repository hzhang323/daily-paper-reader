Abstract: Factor modeling provides a framework for extracting common compo-nents from high-dimensional time series. We develop an estimator of the factor loading space that combines contemporaneous covariance with lagged autocovari-ance information. Under cross-sectionally heteroskedastic white noise, the con-temporaneous covariance contains heterogeneous diagonal noise contributions. We apply the existing HeteroPCA algorithm to the combined estimation matrix to correct this contamination while retaining information from serial dependence. The framework also accommodates noise contamination on a known set of entries subject to sparsity and incoherence conditions. Under regularity conditions for dependent observations, we establish consistency and derive convergence rates, identifying regimes in which incorporating contemporaneous covariance improves the rate relative to the lagged-autocovariance estimator. Simulations evaluate loading-space estimation error and the effects of contemporaneous covariance inclusion and diagonal correction. An application to S&P 500 stock returns il-lustrates the method. 

Key words and phrases: Factor models, heteroskedastic noise, HeteroPCA, loading-space estimation, time series. 

1. Introduction 

The modern capacity for data collection has led to an abundance of time series data, with high-dimensional time series becoming increasingly prevalent across diverse fields such as economics, finance, engineering, en-vironmental science, medical research, network traffic monitoring, image processing, and beyond. Factor modeling has emerged as a powerful tool for analyzing such data from a dimension-reduction perspective, with foun-dational contributions including Bai and Ng (2002), Bai (2003), and Stock and Watson (2005). Related work studies dynamic specifications and the temporal structure of common components (e.g., Molenaar (1985), Tiao and Tsay (1989), Barigozzi and Hallin (2016)). A critical component in constructing a factor model is determining the number of factors, a topic that has been extensively studied in the context of vector factor models (see 1arXiv:2610.00766v1 [stat.ME] 30 Sep 2026 HETEROSKEDASTIC TIME-SERIES FACTOR MODELS 

Bai and Ng (2002), Lam and Yao (2012), Hallin and Liˇ ska (2007), Amen-gual and Watson (2007)). More recently, this line of research has extended to matrix factor models (Yu et al. (2022), Chen and Fan (2023), Wang, Liu and Chen (2019), Yu et al. (2024)) and tensor factor models (Chen and Lam (2024), Chen, Han and Yu (2026), Chen, Yang and Zhang (2022)). While numerous studies have explored various forms of dynamic fac-tor models—including vector, matrix, and tensor formulations—the lagged autocovariance framework of Lam and Yao (2012) does not exploit the po-tentially informative contemporaneous covariance. In this work, we focus on the vector factor model and study how contemporaneous and lagged covariance information can be used jointly for loading-space estimation. Considering the factor model proposed by Lam and Yao (2012), we ob-serve a linear dynamic structure in the process yt, which can be decomposed as: 

yt = Ax t + ϵt, (1.1) where xt is an unobserved r × 1 latent factor process with r ≤ p, A is an unknown p × r loading matrix of full column rank, and ϵt ∼ W N (μϵ, Σϵ)is a white-noise vector process. This model achieves effective dimension reduction when r ≪ p, and the process xt is referred to as the factor process. The key objective in inference is to estimate the factor loading space M(A), defined as the r-dimensional linear subspace spanned by the columns of A. Although the loading matrix depends on the choice of factor coordinates, its column space is invariant under invertible transformations of those coordinates. Define Σy(k) = Cov( yt+k, yt), Σx(k) = Cov( xt+k, xt), and Σxϵ (k) = Cov( xt+k, ϵt) for k ≥ 0. Under the standard assumption that future white noise is uncorrelated with current and past factors, for k ≥ 1 we have Σy(k) = A{Σx(k)A⊤ + Σxϵ (k)}, and hence M{ Σy(k)} ⊆ M (A) for each k ≥ 1. The primary challenge in this extension is heteroskedastic noise: the entries of the noise vector ϵt may have substantially different variances, which contaminate the diagonal of the contemporaneous covariance. Heteroskedasticity has long been studied in approximate factor mod-els, including through likelihood-based estimation (Bai and Liao, 2016). For principal subspace estimation, Zhang, Cai and Wu (2022) introduced HeteroPCA, which iteratively imputes the diagonal of a low-rank signal matrix using its off-diagonal structure. Related developments include in-ference with missing data (Yan, Chen and Fan, 2024), Deflated HeteroPCA (Zhou and Chen, 2025), and diagonal-deletion approaches (Lounici, 2013; Cai et al., 2021). A particularly relevant application is Kaebi (2026), who applies Het-eroPCA to the contemporaneous sample covariance of financial returns and evaluates the resulting latent factors for asset pricing. That covariance-based construction uses bΣy(0) as the input to HeteroPCA. Our estimator instead applies HeteroPCA to cM ∗ = bΣy(0) + cM , incorporating serial de-pendence directly into the matrix used for loading-space estimation. This distinction concerns the information used by the estimator; using time-indexed observations or rolling estimation windows does not by itself in-troduce lagged autocovariances. HeteroPCA applied to contemporaneous covariance alone is therefore a distinct and relevant benchmark. The com-parison with the lagged-only estimator studied here addresses the benefit of adding contemporaneous information and does not, by itself, establish superiority over that contemporaneous-only benchmark. Motivated by this distinction, we incorporate HeteroPCA (Zhang, Cai and Wu, 2022) into the lagged-autocovariance framework of Lam and Yao (2012). We are not aware of existing work that combines HeteroPCA with this framework to estimate the loading space of high-dimensional vector time series from contemporaneous and lagged covariances jointly. The methodological contribution lies in the joint time-series estimation matrix, 3HETEROSKEDASTIC TIME-SERIES FACTOR MODELS 

its theoretical analysis under dependent observations, and the tradeoff be-tween contemporaneous and lagged signal strengths; the HeteroPCA im-putation algorithms themselves are taken from Zhang, Cai and Wu (2022). The main contributions are fourfold. First, we construct a joint estima-tion matrix that augments the Lam–Yao lagged-autocovariance matrix with contemporaneous covariance. Second, we apply HeteroPCA to correct the heterogeneous diagonal noise contributions in this matrix. Third, we ap-ply the existing Generalized HeteroPCA algorithm of Zhang, Cai and Wu (2022) to the joint time-series estimation matrix when the corrupted en-tries lie in a known set, under sparsity and incoherence conditions. This extension concerns the time-series estimation problem rather than a new imputation algorithm. Fourth, under assumptions on temporal dependence and signal strength, we establish consistency and derive convergence rates, identifying regimes in which adding contemporaneous covariance improves the rate relative to the lagged-autocovariance estimator. The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 intro-duces the model and outlines our estimation procedure, which incorporates both contemporaneous and lagged covariance structures via the HeteroPCA algorithm. It also describes the application of the existing Generalized Het-eroPCA algorithm to noise contamination on a known set of entries. Sec-tion 3 establishes the theoretical properties of our estimator and compares its convergence rate with that of the lagged-autocovariance estimator. Sec-tion 4 reports two simulation studies comparing the empirical performance of our method with existing approaches. In Section 5, we apply our method to S&P 500 stock returns and compare the estimated loading-space struc-tures. Finally, Section 6 concludes the paper.

4. Simulation 

We consider two simulation settings: (i) increasing sample size n with fixed dimension p and factor number r; and (ii) increasing n and p with fixed r. The comparisons are designed to assess the contribution of con-temporaneous and lagged covariance information, as well as the effect of correcting heteroskedastic noise. We introduce four loading-space estimators within a common compar-ison framework. Our proposed estimator bAH applies HeteroPCA to the joint matrix cM ∗ and is defined in Definition 2.1. The three benchmarks are bAP , obtained by uncorrected PCA of cM ∗; bAM , the lagged-only esti-mator of Lam and Yao (2012) based on cM ; and bA0, obtained by applying HeteroPCA to the contemporaneous sample covariance bΣy(0), following the covariance-based construction in Kaebi (2026). Their definitions are given together below. The definitions use a common rank ˆ r, which can be selected by the eigenvalue-ratio rule in (2.2), and a common iteration limit T for the two HeteroPCA estimators. In the simulations below, we instead set ˆ r = r

to isolate loading-space estimation from factor-number selection. 

Definition 4.1 ( bAP ). We define bAP ∈ Rp×ˆr as the matrix whose columns are orthonormal eigenvectors of cM ∗ corresponding to its ˆr largest eigenval-ues. No diagonal correction is applied. 

Definition 4.2 ( bAM ). We define bAM ∈ Rp×ˆr as the matrix whose columns are orthonormal eigenvectors of cM corresponding to its ˆr largest eigenval-ues. Its input matrix uses only positive-lag sample autocovariances. 

Definition 4.3 ( bA0). Apply Algorithm 1 with input matrix bΣy(0) in place of cM ∗, rank ˆr, and iteration limit T . We define bA0 ∈ Rp×ˆr as the ma-trix whose columns are the resulting orthonormal loading-space estimates. Conditional on the chosen rank, its matrix input contains no positive-lag autocovariances.

6. Discussion 

This paper integrates contemporaneous covariance and lagged autoco-variance information into loading-space estimation by applying HeteroPCA to a joint estimation matrix. This construction retains serial dependence information while correcting heterogeneous diagonal noise contamination. Under the stated assumptions, we establish consistency and convergence-rate bounds for dependent observations, identifying signal-strength regimes in which incorporating contemporaneous information improves the upper bound relative to the lagged-only estimator. The analysis also accommo-dates structured non-diagonal noise with known support through General-ized HeteroPCA. Simulations and an application to stock returns illustrate the estimation performance and loading-space structure of the proposed 21 HETEROSKEDASTIC TIME-SERIES FACTOR MODELS 

approach. Several directions merit further investigation. Broader comparisons with alternative heteroskedastic factor estimators and low-rank recovery methods would help clarify the relative strengths of the proposed approach across noise and dependence structures. Such comparisons could assess both loading-space estimation and out-of-sample prediction. Other directions in-clude data-adaptive weighting of contemporaneous and lagged information, joint selection of the factor number and lag order, and extensions to matrix-and tensor-valued time series.

rank( M ) = r, G ⊆ [m1] × [m2], max i |{ j : ( i, j ) ∈ G}| ≤ b, max j |{ i : ( i, j ) ∈G}| ≤ b, then we have 

∥G(M )∥ ≤ √b ∧ r∥M ∥, ∥Γ( M )∥ ≤ (√b ∧ r + 1) ∥M ∥.

In particular, if M ∈ Rp×p is any square matrix and ∆( M ) is the matrix 

M with diagonal entries set to 0, then 

∥∆( M )∥ ≤ 2∥M ∥.

Here, the factor “2” in the statement above cannot be improved.
