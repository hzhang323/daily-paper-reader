Abstract 

Temporal reports are increasingly emitted alongside numerical forecasts and are often interpreted as statements about the computation producing those forecasts. We formalize the resulting certification problem as three distinct stages: recoverability , report correctness , and functional use . For point delays, an exact finite-sample recovery–substitutability identity ties structural discrimination and proxy prediction to the same realized shift geometry while placing them on different scales: structural evidence grows with 

nη n, whereas the normalized predictive penalty is governed by ηn. A delay can therefore be statistically decisive while an alternative lag remains near-oracle. Guided by this regime, we evaluate TCN- and N-HiTS-based systems on the strict intersection of recoverable trajectories, correct reports, and near-oracle one-step predictions. Their dominant forecast dependence remains far from the reported delay under masking, finite perturbations, local Jacobians, and in-distribution conditional replacement, and the separation persists across a 100-fold forecast-loss sweep. A no-bypass factorization then provides an explicit access certificate for the final stage, while architecture-matched multi-seed, post-hoc, and gate-destruction controls identify report-coordinate access as the mechanism governing alignment. The resulting framework separates statistical evidence for what can be identified from computational evidence for what the forecast uses .

# 1 Introduction 

Temporal structure is increasingly exposed alongside forecasts: a system may name a delay, leading indicator, selected history window, or distributed temporal pattern in addition to producing a numerical prediction. Such reports carry two different kinds of information. They may identify a property that is supported by the observed trajectory, and they may describe the computation that actually produced the forecast. Treating these claims as interchangeable turns a readable structural output into a stronger computational certificate than the evidence warrants. This paper develops the statistical and functional conditions needed to separate them. Throughout, the neural systems are forecasting backbones equipped with an explicit temporal-structure report ; the report is an evaluated model output rather than an assumed property of a vanilla TCN or N-HiTS. We organize temporal-report evidence into a three-stage certificate hierarchy. Recoverability asks whether the realized trajectory contains enough information to distinguish the target structure from alternatives. 

Report correctness asks whether the fitted system names that structure correctly. Functional use asks whether the numerical prediction depends on the history named by the report. The stages are evaluated in the order recoverability −→ report correctness −→ functional use ,

with each stage supplying evidence of a different kind. Autocorrelation makes the hierarchy consequential: nearby lags can be predictive substitutes, output memory can compensate for an incorrect input delay, and 1arXiv:2608.10433v2 [cs.LG] 13 Aug 2026 accurate forecasts can be supported by history different from the generating mechanism [Bj¨ orklund and Ljung, 2003, Sadler and Kozick, 2006, Ljung, 1999, Seborg et al., 2016, Kay, 1993]. A temporal report is therefore computationally informative only when structural evidence is connected to the forecast map itself. The theoretical core identifies the statistical reason that this connection cannot be inferred from structural correctness alone. For point delays, structural discrimination and predictive substitution are governed by the 

same realized shift geometry , but that geometry enters the two problems at different scales. If ηn denotes the normalized prediction penalty of a wrong-lag proxy, trajectory-level structural evidence grows with nη n

while proxy prediction cost remains at scale ηn. This yields an exact finite-sample certificate gap: the true delay can be statistically decisive while a correlated alternative remains within a fixed near-oracle tolerance. In the local-substitutability regime, structural error can vanish even as the proxy excess risk itself tends to zero. The fixed-process and local asymptotic consequences make this separation a property of the statistical problem, not a peculiarity of a particular neural architecture. The empirical study instantiates the same hierarchy in learned forecasters. The primary Link-II evaluation fixes the first two stages and forecast quality simultaneously by restricting to P1 trajectories with Bayes risk at most .05 , a correct explicit report, and one-step normalized error NER ≤ .10 . On these rows, TCN- and N-HiTS-based systems still place their dominant numerical dependence far from the reported delay. The discrepancy appears under zero masking, finite perturbations, local Jacobians, and conditional replacement from the exact white/AR(1) Gaussian input law, and it remains substantial across forecast-loss coefficients 

.1, 1, and 10 . The empirical result therefore measures the remaining computational question after statistical identifiability, structural reporting, and near-oracle prediction have already been established on the evaluated instance. 

Contributions. Our contributions are threefold. 1. A certificate hierarchy for temporal reports. We formalize recoverability, report correctness, and functional use as distinct evidential objects, giving temporal explanation a statistical-to-computational evaluation order rather than a single accuracy criterion. 2. An exact recovery–substitutability law. For point delays we derive a finite-sample identity, together with fixed-process and local-asymptotic consequences, showing that the same realized temporal geometry controls structural identification at scale nη n and predictive substitutability at scale ηn.3. A controlled realization of the certificate gap and an access certificate for closure. On recoverable, correct-report, near-oracle cases, multiple intervention diagnostics locate forecast dependence away from the report. A no-bypass factorization then turns report-conditioned access into a verifiable computational property; architecture-matched multi-seed, post-hoc, and gate-destruction controls isolate coordinate access as the mechanism driving alignment. 

Central principle. The resulting principle is that statistical evidence and computational evidence answer different questions. Recoverability certifies that a structural question is answerable from the observed trajectory; report correctness certifies that the fitted system answers that question correctly; functional-use analysis certifies how that answer is connected to the numerical forecast. The recovery–substitutability identity links the first and third stages through a common geometry while proving that their statistical scales differ. The no-bypass construction supplies the complementary computational certificate by making access through the reported structure explicit. Together, these pieces turn temporal reporting from a single-output interpretation problem into a staged theory of what can be inferred about a forecaster.

# 2 Related Work 

Recoverability and temporal structure. Delay and lead–lag estimation are classical in system identifica-tion and remain central in modern time-series analysis [Bj¨ orklund and Ljung, 2003, Sadler and Kozick, 2006, Ljung, 1999, Ma and Huang, 2023, Cattaldo et al., 2024, Heyse et al., 2021, Zhao and Shen, 2024, Runge et al., 2019a,b, Runge, 2020]. Recent work studies when temporal structure is identifiable or recoverable from the observed process [Kuskova et al., 2026b, Tan et al., 2025]. We use recoverability as a trajectory-level certificate: complete candidate likelihoods give input-conditioned Bayes limits for P1/P3, while nuisance-profile separation handles P2 settings in which an incorrect lag can refit autoregressive dynamics. This places structural reporting on top of an explicit statistical information limit. 

Faithfulness and temporal explanations. A readable model output and the computation producing a prediction are distinct objects in the broader faithfulness literature [Jain and Wallace, 2019, Jacovi and Goldberg, 2020, Ross et al., 2017]. Time-series explanation methods use perturbations, context windows, predictive masks, information bottlenecks, and forecast-necessity criteria to characterize temporal dependence [Ozyegen et al., 2022, Liu et al., 2024c,b, Kim et al., 2026, Zhang et al., 2025, Kuskova et al., 2026a, Zheng et al., 2026]; autocorrelation can distort marginal temporal attribution [Tunyi, 2026]. Our setting links this functional question to a separately evaluated structural claim. Conditional replacement under the known Gaussian input law complements masking, finite perturbations, and Jacobians by measuring the same report–forecast relation under an in-distribution intervention. 

Bottlenecks and controlled benchmarks. Concept bottlenecks and related inherently faithful architectures place an interpretable variable on the prediction path [Koh et al., 2020]. We use the same architectural idea as a computational certificate : the no-bypass factorization bounds off-report access, and matched post-hoc and gate-destruction controls identify coordinate access as the operative mechanism. Controlled synthetic and causal benchmarks provide the known structural targets required to score both statistical recovery and model 3response [Cheng et al., 2024, Mogensen et al., 2024, Stein et al., 2025, Herdeanu et al., 2025, Yi et al., 2026, Zhao et al., 2026]. 

Statistical and computational certificates. The paper’s contribution lies in the relation among these lines of work. Recoverability, structural reporting, and functional dependence are placed in one ordered certificate hierarchy, and the recovery–substitutability identity gives an exact statistical law connecting the first and third stages. Because the same realized shift geometry appears as nη n in structural evidence and ηn in normalized predictive cost, reliable identification and cheap predictive substitution can coexist on the same trajectory. The experiments realize this regime in learned forecasters after fixing recoverability, report correctness, and near-oracle prediction, while the routed factorization provides an explicit access certificate for the final stage.

# 4 Theory 

The theory develops the two certificates needed by the hierarchy. The statistical certificate is determined by how strongly the realized trajectory separates structural candidates; predictive substitutability is determined by the cost of replacing the target history with an alternative. The central identity shows that these quantities share one geometry but use different normalizations, creating a regime in which structural identification is decisive while proxy prediction remains near-oracle. The second part formalizes a computational access certificate: a report-conditioned no-bypass factorization converts the final report–use relation into a property of the prediction map. Full statements and proofs are collected in Sections B and C.

# 5 Experiments 

The experiments evaluate the certificate hierarchy in the same order as the theory. TCN [Bai et al., 2018] and N-HiTS [Challu et al., 2023] are the primary backbones, each equipped with the explicit structural report defined in Section 3; matched statistical references quantify recoverability for each mechanism. The unified cohort contains 400 trajectories. The architecture-matched access study uses an independent 1,200-trajectory P1 cohort and ten fixed training seeds, and the P2 mechanism replication uses a second independent 1,200-trajectory cohort. All paired uncertainty preserves trajectory identity, and crossed seed–trajectory rows are treated as repeated evaluations rather than independent samples. 

5.1 Recoverability and forecasting answer different questions 

The first stage asks whether a structural error reflects the fitted system or ambiguity in the realized data. P1 Bayes calculations agree with Monte Carlo, the P2 profile score agrees with direct optimization to approximately 10 −15 relative error, and P3 finite-library error is ordered by the numerical Bayes reference (Appendix G). On the common cohort, the matched references approach the structural limit in the recoverable region while the neural readouts leave substantial headroom. Forecast quality and structural recovery exhibit different orderings. On P2 and P3, N-HiTS has lower forecast MSE while TCN has lower structural error; the paired ranking contrasts are statistically resolved (Appendix G.12). Structural recovery improves further with training budget (Appendix G.10). Forecast accuracy and structural recovery therefore occupy distinct axes of the certificate hierarchy even before functional use is evaluated.

5.4 Robustness beyond the core setting 

The certificate hierarchy extends beyond the core P1 experiment. P2 adds autoregressive output memory and nuisance refitting, P3 replaces a point delay by distributed structure, and an independent iTransformer study broadens the architecture class. Controlled misspecification, structural OOD, and semi-synthetic hydraulic and Beijing input histories introduce substantially different temporal geometries. Across these settings, recoverability, report correctness, and functional response remain separately measurable; the matched access-control mechanism recurs beyond P1, including the P2 post-hoc decomposition and real-input routing contrasts. Full results are in Appendix G. 

# 6 Discussion and Conclusion 

Temporal reporting admits a natural certificate hierarchy: the data must support a structural distinction, the fitted system must report that structure correctly, and the numerical forecast must be connected to the reported history. The recovery–substitutability identity supplies the statistical law linking these stages. Because the same realized temporal geometry appears as nη n in structural evidence and ηn in normalized proxy cost, statistical certainty about a delay can coexist with inexpensive predictive substitution. Structural correctness and computational necessity are therefore different properties even when they arise from the same observed trajectory. The learned-model results realize this certificate gap directly. On trajectories that are recoverable, correctly reported, and near-oracle in one-step prediction, TCN- and N-HiTS-based systems retain large 10 report–use gaps across masking, finite perturbations, Jacobians, and in-distribution conditional replacement, with the same separation visible across a 100-fold forecast-loss sweep. The no-bypass factorization supplies the complementary computational side of the theory: report-conditioned access makes off-report sensitivity explicitly controllable. Architecture-matched multi-seed comparisons, within-checkpoint gating, gate de-struction, and P2 replication localize the alignment effect to that access mechanism. The resulting framework gives temporal reports a precise evidential meaning—recoverability certifies what the trajectory can reveal, report correctness certifies what the system identifies, and access-sensitive functional analysis certifies what the forecast computation actually uses. 11 AI use statement

Candidate, input, and prior contract. The candidate index H is independent of the input mechanism in the core synthetic tier: U ⊥ H. Exact Bayes statements condition on every boundary variable used by the candidate likelihood. If an input policy or initial-history law depends on the candidate, the structural prior is conditioned on the observed variables and the corresponding likelihood contribution is included. P2 profile calibration uses the common-information window in Equation (1), which excludes the transition whose burn-in boundary value is not part of the observed history. 

Innovation contract. P1 and P3 assume independent Gaussian innovations. P2 assumes ϵt is independent of the filtration generated by past output and the observed exogenous input. Closed-loop inputs are excluded from the exact P2 theorem unless the controller and likelihood are explicitly modeled. 

Boundary history and the P2 common-information window. The general P2/P3 conditional formulas require the boundary output history used by the recursion to be observed and conditioned upon consistently. For P2, the first scored transition depends on a burn-in boundary value that is not part of the serialized observed history. The common-information analysis therefore starts at L + 2 , where YL+1 is observed, and uses nP2 = T − L − 1. This is the declared evaluation window for all reported P2 profile calibration. The implemented exact P3 cohort has a = 0 , so no output-boundary history enters its KL/Bayes computation. For a future nonzero-a exact P3 cohort, the common observed boundary history would have to be conditioned upon explicitly. 

Known versus unknown nuisance parameters. A known-parameter Bayes oracle defines a strict same-information limit only when those parameters are fixed and included in the observed information set for every evaluated system. If the parameters are hidden or vary within the scenario, the known-parameter result is a labeled lower reference. Profile likelihood is a feasible reference when gain, previous-output carry-over, or variance are unknown. Profile-KL is a population separation diagnostic for a composite incorrect candidate; it is not, by itself, an exact composite-hypothesis Bayes risk. 

Model/report computational contract. For the separate-head neural evaluation, the scientific contract is functional rather than architecture-name specific: the numerical forecast is computed without taking the realized report r or a report-induced historical gate as an input. The report is a declarative estimate of temporal structure, not an instruction that must be fed back into the predictor. A report head may share upstream features or parameters with the forecaster, but changing the report value while holding the forecast-path state fixed does not alter the forecast forward pass. In routed regimes, the report induces the gate controlling access within the audited historical block as in Equation (9). These two contracts instantiate, respectively, the unconstrained report–use test and a block-conditional no-bypass certificate. For every separate-head P1–P3 run reported from the unified release, TCN and N-HiTS use the literal objective in the objective Lsep = 0 .1 MSE( by, y ) + CE( z, c ): one-step forecast MSE has weight 0.1 and report cross-entropy has weight 1.0. AdamW uses learning rate 10 −3, batch size 128, 12 epochs, and gradient-norm clipping at 1.0. Both losses update the shared backbone; only MSE updates the scalar forecast head, and only cross-entropy updates the report head. Thus the report objective improves the report without placing the realized report upstream of the forecast. # E Detailed Synthetic Generator Specification 

E.1 Unified generation order 

For each trajectory, the implementation follows the fixed order: 1. sample the input family and generate U1: Traw ;2. sample the structural candidate and dynamic nuisance parameters; 3. generate the clean latent process and apply burn-in removal; 23 4. generate innovations and observation corruption using independent random streams; 5. crop every candidate to the common task window; for P2 profile calibration, additionally drop the first post-boundary transition and use IP2  

> L

so that every lagged output regressor is actually observed; 6. compute P1/P3 KL/Bayes quantities and the P2 common-information finite-sample profile quantities on their declared windows; exact models with nonzero output memory must condition on any required observed boundary history; 7. assign a difficulty bin and write the complete metadata record; 8. split by complete trajectory and parameter-combination group. The raw length includes a burn-in chosen from the slowest declared state decay and the maximum candidate lag. Numerical stationarity is checked empirically in addition to analytic stability conditions. 

E.2 Reference configuration 

Unless a factor is being scanned, the reference instance uses 

T = 512 , d = 8 , SNR = 10 dB , ρU = 0 .5, a = 0 .6. (34) For P3, the reference kernel is a normalized two-path kernel with spacing four and secondary mass 0.3. The reference stress track uses Gaussian innovations and no corruption; each stress experiment changes one declared module. 

E.3 Stable ARX(2) templates 

Rather than sampling arbitrary coefficients, the initial release uses a small validated template library. Each pair 

(a1, a 2) is accepted only if the roots of the state-transition polynomial imply a stable recursion. Templates are selected to produce fast monotone, slow monotone, and mildly oscillatory impulse responses. The exact released coefficients and their pole locations are stored in the scenario configuration. 

E.4 Kernel construction 

Point, multipath, triangular, exponential, and biphasic kernels are first constructed on support {0, . . . , L } and then normalized. For two-path kernels, with overall kernel gain bh and secondary-path weight w ∈ [0 , 1] ,

hk = bh [(1 − w)1{k = d1} + w1{k = d2}] . (35) For exponential kernels with decay constant τh > 0,

hd+ℓ ∝ exp( −ℓ/τ h), ℓ ≥ 0. (36) Matched kernel pairs are accepted only after checking that their parameter distance and realized mean-response distance fall in the pre-specified range. 

E.5 Noise and corruption modules 

Student-t innovations are rescaled to the target variance when the variance exists. Contaminated Gaussian noise uses a stored contamination indicator. Colored innovations are generated before the process recursion and are not confused with output autoregression. Missingness, quantization, and saturation are applied only after a clean latent output has been generated. Both clean and corrupted outputs are released. 24 E.6 Physics-inspired modules 

Cascade, parallel-path, and sensor-smoothing systems are simulated in state-space form with explicit states. The benchmark label contains both the physical path delays and an optional summary kernel derived from an impulse response. Structural scoring uses the physical labels; functional scoring may additionally use the realized impulse-response kernel. This prevents an effective-lag summary from replacing the declared physical target. 

E.7 Generator validation 

Each configuration must pass: • stability and finite-value checks; • target versus empirical SNR checks; • candidate-label independence from input metadata and array shape; • empirical autocorrelation and spectrum sanity checks; • impulse-response verification for every mechanism family; • paired-noise reproducibility and random-seed replay; • shortcut tests using input-only and metadata-only classifiers. A scenario family is excluded from the main benchmark if a metadata-only classifier predicts its candidate label above the pre-specified tolerance. 

# F Detailed Experimental Sampling Protocol 

F.1 One-factor and interaction sweeps 

The main severity study varies one factor from the reference configuration. A fixed set of two-factor interactions is also evaluated for robustness; the exact interaction definitions and parameter ranges are part of the released scenario configuration rather than introduced as additional paper notation. All remaining combinations are sampled from a fixed configuration distribution and are used for robustness rather than exhaustive factorial claims. 

F.2 Difficulty-targeted sampling 

For exact-tier instances, a proposal is repeatedly generated until its oracle quantity falls in the target bin. The proposal distribution and maximum rejection count are fixed before benchmark execution. If a bin cannot be populated without extreme parameters, the bin is marked unsupported rather than filled by changing its definition. 

F.3 Minimum reporting units

G.5 Functional consistency and diagnostic calibration 

Functional-use terminology. Throughout the paper, “use” denotes controlled sensitivity of the fitted numerical predictor to historical input locations under fixed intervention diagnostics; it is an operational notion of functional dependence rather than a claim of real-world causality or complete mechanistic equivalence. For the binary P1 evaluation, “unused” is the matched-window masking label defined in the main text: the reported ±1-lag window is zeroed and compared with 20 disjoint equal-width energy-matched control windows. This label is computed before any conditioning on report correctness or NER and is held fixed in the triple-separation analysis. Energy matching reduces a simple scale confound but does not make the zeroed sequence conditionally in-distribution; for that reason the paper treats masking as one diagnostic rather than a unique ground truth. Additive perturbations and, especially, local input Jacobians provide continuous checks 29 that do not depend on the binary masking threshold. The target profile is tied to a known mechanism because the exact and physics-inspired tiers expose the declared delay or kernel used by the generator. Before interpreting learned-model scores, we test the diagnostics on seven predictors with known functional structure: a true-delay oracle, a deliberately shifted delay, a true two-path rule, a similar-error wrong kernel, an output-only rule, a uniform-history rule, and an exact hard one-hot replay. On 400 fresh trajectories per predictor, additive sensitivity and input Jacobians recover nonuniform full profiles with cosine similarity numerically equal to one and zero peak error, while all three diagnostics correctly reject input use for the output-only predictor. Matched donor-block replacement localizes single paths to within 0.85–0.88 lags on average, but recalls only 0.620 of weak secondary paths for the two-path rule and 0.423 for hard one-hot replay. It is therefore retained as a qualitative robustness check, not the primary full-kernel diagnostic; its uniform-history blind spot is likewise explicitly recorded.
