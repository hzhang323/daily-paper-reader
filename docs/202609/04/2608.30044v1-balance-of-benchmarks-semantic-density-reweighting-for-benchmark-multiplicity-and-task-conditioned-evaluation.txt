## ABSTRACT 

Language models are commonly compared by averaging scores across a bench-mark list with equal weight. Such lists grow through publication outside an explicit measurement design, so equal weighting turns the density of published benchmarks into an implicit capability weight: densely benchmarked regions count repeatedly. We introduce Balance of Benchmarks (BoB), which embeds benchmark descriptions and assigns each benchmark an inverse-density seman-tic weight. Nearby entries share aggregate influence at a disclosed density scale. After equating heterogeneous scores onto a common latent scale, a residual field uses the same geometry to condition model rankings on a task query. The two components serve distinct empirical roles. On a snapshot of 586 models and 14 benchmarks, BoB predicts which models are unusually strong on a held-out task beyond their general ability, reaching a profile correlation of 0.462 compared with 0.049 under equal weighting. It also limits the influence of densely repeated benchmarks on the aggregate. After adding four copies of each benchmark in turn, the resulting rankings retain Kendall τ = 0 .995 , compared with 0.936 under equal weighting. The residual field therefore provides task-conditioned prediction, and inverse-density weighting provides robustness to benchmark multiplicity. To-gether, they turn benchmark-list composition from an incidental property of eval-uation suites into an explicit, controllable part of measurement design, providing a principled foundation for task-aware and multiplicity-robust model evaluation. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

A leaderboard’s headline number is a weighted average, and its weights are usually equal. Equal weights appear neutral at the level of benchmark names, yet they imply a consequential choice at the level of capabilities: a region of capability space receives weight in proportion to the number of published benchmarks that happen to sample it. Benchmark lists grow by accretion, so publica-tion density becomes capability weight. In the Artificial Analysis snapshot studied here, AIME and AIME’25 lie at angular distance 0.29 in an embedding of their descriptions, and the two Terminal-Bench variants at 0.36, against a median pairwise distance of 0.89 (Figure 1a). Competition mathe-matics and terminal agency therefore enter an equal-weight average more than once, with no explicit decision that they should matter more. Existing aggregation strategies expose the same problem in different forms. Hand-assigned cate-gories require discrete boundaries and give every benchmark inside a category the same relation to its neighbours. Weights estimated from cross-benchmark score covariance adapt to redundancy in the current model population, but the resulting scale changes when the population changes and can mistake common optimization or contamination for redundant coverage. Subset-selection methods ask which benchmarks add new information, but a leaderboard that retains the full list still needs continuous weights. The missing object is a model of how the benchmark list samples the task space. Balance of Benchmarks (BoB) represents each benchmark by an embedded description and uses local description density to assign continuous weights over the observed list. Contributions. (i) Benchmark multiplicity as an aggregation problem. We show that equal weighting turns the number of published benchmarks per capability into an implicit capability weight and make benchmark-list composition an explicit part of aggregation design. (ii) Continuous inverse-density reweighting. Description-derived weights smoothly discount densely represented neighbourhoods with no category boundaries or duplicate threshold. The marginal-mass formula connects semantic densification to empirical robustness under re-listing. (iii) Task-conditioned evaluation. A query-conditioned residual field uses the same geometry to identify models that out-perform their general ability near a task. On 586 models and 14 benchmarks, BoB reaches 0.902 level and 0.462 profile correlation in held-out prediction. Shuffled descriptions reach 0.336 profile on average and 0.430 at most, isolating the additional increment from correct semantic placement. 

## 2 RELATED WORK 

Leaderboard aggregation. Holistic suites and arenas made breadth a design goal while leaving the final aggregate to curation (Liang et al., 2023; Srivastava et al., 2023; Chiang et al., 2024; Singh et al., 2025; Artificial Analysis, 2026; Colombo et al., 2022). Under equal weighting, however, the 2Preprint. number of benchmarks assigned to a capability determines its contribution to the aggregate. Hand-maintained taxonomies make that choice explicit, but introduce category boundaries and mainte-nance costs (Hudson, 2026). BoB models the composition of the benchmark list directly, treating its entries as samples from a continuous task space. 

Benchmark redundancy and selection. Score-side methods measure benchmark agreement, di-mensionality, and predictability (Perlitz et al., 2024b; Ruan et al., 2024; Sha & Zhao, 2026). Related methods compress evaluation while preserving model rankings (Perlitz et al., 2024a; Maia Polo et al., 2024; Kipnis et al., 2025; Vivek et al., 2024). These estimates depend on the evaluated model population. A complementary line frames subset choice as marginal coverage, using description embeddings or score-side information (Yao et al., 2026; Smola, 2026). BoB carries the marginal-coverage view from subset choice to continuous weighting of the full list, with weights derived from descriptions and therefore exogenous to the population being ranked. 

Semantic task representations. Representations of models and tasks support score prediction and model routing, including for unseen tasks (Zhuang et al., 2025; Song et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025; Ge et al., 2026; Park et al., 2025; Li, 2025). BoB uses the same locality principle to return a task-conditioned ranking: nearby benchmark residuals adjust a global ability estimate. Because bench-mark scores differ in difficulty and saturation, characteristic curves first place them on a common latent scale, following score-equating ideas from item response theory (Lalor et al., 2016; Rodriguez et al., 2021; Lord & Novick, 1968; Stocking & Lord, 1983; Kolen & Brennan, 2014). This calibra-tion supplies comparable residuals. Inverse-density weighting and the query-conditioned residual field provide the aggregation mechanisms. 

## 3 METHOD 

BoB assumes that benchmark descriptions locate what their evaluations measure: benchmarks prob-ing similar capabilities lie near one another in a semantic embedding, and a model’s performance varies smoothly over that space. Score equating places heterogeneous bench-marks on a common ability scale (Section 3.1). Inverse-density weighting adjusts each benchmark’s influence according to local description density (Section 3.2). A residual field conditions the ag-gregate on a task query (Section 3.3). ## 5 LIMITATIONS 

Semantic proximity is an imperfect proxy for redundant coverage. A small distance can de-scribe a re-listing, a fresh item sample from the same design, or an independent benchmark of the same capability. BoB assigns these cases the same geometric treatment, because using their score covariance would make the weight population-dependent. The representation can also miss a rela-tionship. The τ 2-Bench and τ -Bench Banking descriptions lie at distance 0.509, moving to 0.647 when their lineage sentence is removed. Their raw pair effective mass is 1.85. Global normalization gives them 2.08 equal-weight benchmark units, so the final aggregate does not discount the pair rela-tive to equal weighting. A richer representation of capability overlap is the main route for improving this boundary. 

The semantic distribution is representation dependent. The inverse-density weights define a distribution over one embedded point per benchmark at a chosen bandwidth. This benchmark-level representation leaves internal task extent and suite packaging outside the current estimator. De-ployment settings can supply non-uniform target weights when particular capabilities have greater operational importance. All distances here come from one embedding family, and the evaluation contains 14 benchmarks. Three embedding sizes and paraphrased descriptions preserve the reported results within this representation family. The shuffled-description control bounds the semantic attri-bution. Generic residual pooling reaches 0.336 profile on average, while correct placement raises it to 0.462. 

Re-listing robustness after refitting. The weighting result controls the mass assigned to co-located points, exactly for an isolated group and up to the similarity it shares with neighbours. Refitting characteristic curves and robust cell weights propagates the remaining change into model abilities. Across 14 four-copy scenarios, the average within-scenario median and 95th-percentile displacements are 0.5 and 4.3 ranks, and the mean maximum is 16.6. These are small relative to equal weighting but are not exact ranking invariance. 

Held-out benchmarks are the easiest task-query regime. The task-conditioned rankings have no direct ground truth for arbitrary user tasks, so we evaluate them by querying a held-out benchmark description. Such a query is drawn from the same construction process as the remaining benchmark descriptions and is typically closer to the observed list than an unconstrained user request. External task distributions and prospective model selection are needed to test the method in deployment. 

## 6 CONCLUSION 

Equal weighting makes the density of published benchmarks part of the measurement, even when that density was never chosen. BoB makes the choice explicit: score equating places heterogeneous benchmarks on a common scale, inverse-density weighting adjusts the influence of densely repre-sented semantic neighbourhoods, and the same task space conditions an aggregate on a user’s query. 8Preprint. The held-out prediction and shuffled-geometry results support task conditioning, while the densifica-tion and duplication experiments identify the distinct role of inverse-density weighting. The residual field supports task-conditioned prediction beyond calibrated general ability. Inverse-density weight-ing stabilizes the aggregate under benchmark multiplicity. Together they expose benchmark-list composition as an explicit measurement decision. REPRODUCIBILITY STATEMENT 

The Artificial Analysis snapshot is fixed at 2026-08-29 and restricted to the 586 models with at least 5 of the 14 benchmarks scored. Every experiment writes a frozen result file. One script regenerates every table from those files and a second regenerates every figure. The accompanying artifact con-tains the anonymized code, frozen results, derived benchmark descriptions, raw task queries, canoni-calized queries, embeddings, fitted curve parameters, and both regeneration scripts. Section G.6 lists every query pair used in the qualitative examples. No model training is involved. Compute consists of embedding inference over short texts followed by CPU fitting of score equating, inverse-density weighting, and the residual field. THE USE OF LARGE LANGUAGE MODELS 

Large language models enter this work in three distinct roles. They are the object of study, since the systems in the evaluation snapshot are language models. They are also a component of the method. The English benchmark descriptions and their paraphrases are generated by a language model from a fixed template. The five demonstration queries are canonicalized into the same descriptive register. These frozen text inputs and the embedding configuration are specified in Section G.3, and canoni-calization is quantified in Section G.5. Finally, language models were used as a writing and coding assistant, for drafting and editing prose and for implementation support, with the authors verifying the output. Every reported number is produced by the released scripts from the frozen result files rather than by a language model. The generated descriptions are not neutral inputs, however. They are the object the geometry is computed on, and four of them originally named their relationship to another benchmark, which Section E tests by removing every such reference and refitting.

## D ABLATIONS AND VALIDITY CHECKS 

Rows A–D of Table 2 are self-contained constructions. They use their construction-specific ability control, whereas the full-method controls use the pool-only evaluation path of the headline exper-iment. The rows order design choices but do not form an additive decomposition. The additional linear z-score and precision-weighted-mean construction reaches 0.014 profile. D.1 GEOMETRY CONTROLS 

Flat weighting. Setting ui ≡ 1 throughout the otherwise unchanged method gives 0.896 level, 0.451 profile, and a duplication shift of 0.973 standard deviations, versus 0.098 for BoB. Its profile is statistically comparable to BoB ( p = 0 .077 ). Inverse-density weighting contributes multiplicity robustness. 

Shuffled descriptions. Reassigning descriptions to benchmarks and rerunning the complete leave-one-benchmark-out procedure 200 times yields profile 0.336 ± 0.042 (maximum 0.430), versus the observed 0.462. This separates the contribution of correct semantic placement from generic pooling of calibrated residuals. 

Residual-to-ability scale. Equation (4) adds an average standardized residual to standardized abil-ity. A first-order latent-offset alternative divides each cell residual by the local derivative of its char-acteristic curve and weights by local information. It gives 0.904 level and 0.435 profile, providing a sensitivity check on the reported direct standardized-residual convention. 

Support-factor specification. Including normalized item information inside Pm(q) gives 0.905 level and 0.460 profile, indistinguishable on profile ( p = 0 .67 ). The conclusion is unchanged when information enters both the residual average and its support factor.
