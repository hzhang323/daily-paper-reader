ABSTRACT 

Time series foundation models (TSFMs) have recently delivered impressive zero-shot performance across diverse forecasting tasks. However, real-world decision-making frequently relies on irregular multivariate time series (IMTS), where in-consistent inter-observation intervals and asynchronous sampling across variables coexist with informative missingness. Existing TSFMs handle such inputs either through imputation that injects spurious values or through index-based positional encodings that ignore continuous time. There is still a gap in the foundation model that follows the original IMTS patterns. In this paper, we propose a hybrid atten-tion model that learns a unified time-aware patch representation for IMTS fore-casting. We first design a time-aware patch encoding that maps a variable number of intra-patch timestamps into a fixed-size embedding, producing a uniform for-mat for irregular patches without resorting to imputation. We then introduce a 

time bias attention mechanism that calibrates inter-patch temporal misalignment and asynchronous cross-channel dependencies as auxiliary attention offset. Fi-nally, on top of a decoder-only Transformer backbone, we adopt a hybrid causal mask that preserves a bidirectional full view over the historical context while keep-ing the forecast horizon strictly autoregressive. To support large-scale pretraining under irregular settings, we also curate VersaTSA, an archive of 30 B observa-tions that retains the native sampling sparsity of its sources. Experiments on three IMTS benchmarks and a standard regular-MTS benchmark show that our model achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot performance on IMTS and remains competitive when transferred to regular forecasting. 

1 INTRODUCTION 

With the advancement of foundational models (FMs) (Bommasani et al., 2022), time series anal-ysis is undergoing a profound transformation. Numerous efforts have been made to establish a unified paradigm for Time Series Foundation models (TSFMs) that possess the exceptional gen-eralization capabilities for multivariate time series forecasting, such as Sundial (Liu et al., 2025), Chronos (Ansari et al., 2024), Moirai (Woo et al., 2024), and TimesFM (Das et al., 2024). However, existing large-scale TSFMs inherently struggle to handle irregularly sampled time series. They re-quire data preprocessing methods such as interpolation to align the data to regular sampling intervals, thereby introducing additional noise (Che et al., 2018) and obscuring informative missingness (RU-BIN, 1976). This approach leads to mismatches when applying these models to real-world scenarios characterized by inherent temporal inconsistencies. Thus, in this paper, we aim to explore how to build a universal model for irregular multivariate time series (IMTS). By investigating irregular time series in various real-world domains, such as financial analysis (Sezer et al., 2020), cyber security (Sousa et al., 2020; Fawaz et al., 2019), and weather science (Ravuri et al., 2021), we can conclude that irregular multivariate time series usually exhibit the following two 1arXiv:2609.22836v1 [cs.LG] 19 Sep 2026 Published as a conference paper at ICLR 2027 𝑡 ! 𝑡 " 𝑡 # 𝑡 $

Existing TSFMs rely on traditional positional encoding (e.g., Sinusoidal (Vaswani et al., 2023), RoPE (Su et al., 2024), T5 Bias (Press et al., 2021)), where they can only encode the sequential order of indices. When temporal intervals are irregular, the positional encoding fails to describe the frequency changes and the natural differences among patches. Concurrently, some mod-els for irregularly sampled time series address the issue by processing data point by point (Rubanova et al., 2019; Luo et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2024). While this approach offers flexibility in handling irregular data, it reduces the information density and increases computational complexity. These cause it to be difficult to achieve positive scaling benefits through model stacking without consider-ing the temporal representation. Thus, an effective time-aware encoding method should be able to follow the time irregularity and represent the frequency changes simultaneously. Based on the above observations, our idea starts from a natural understanding of irregular intervals. Empirically, continuous-time encoding transforms timestamps into a continuous space (Kazemi et al., 2019), enabling models to “perceive” temporal dimensions. Thus, we can build a universal temporal representation learning method based on TSFMs to achieve continuous modeling of times. To achieve this target, the following challenges need to be addressed. First, existing continuous-time encoding approaches predominantly embed time using timestamps as units. But multiple timestamps may exist within a single patch, rendering these methods ineffective. Although the average time en-coding provides a solution to represent the temporal information of patches, the capture of intricate inner-patch temporal structure remains underexplored. Secondly, regarding inter-patch position encoding, conventional methods encode the sequence order of the index. It neglects the uneven-ness between patches . Third, existing base models often employ Decoder-Only Transformers with causal masks to maintain the autoregressive manner. However, inconsistent sampling rates or miss-ing values across different variables lead to overlapping patches during the chunk process, rendering 

causal masking ineffective .To address the above challenges, we propose a hybrid attention model to learn the UNI fied Time-aware patch embeddings for Irregular Multivariate Time Series forecasting (U NI TIMS). These patches are then flattened into a unified sequence (Woo et al., 2024). For each channel, the observed value sequence {u(m−1) ·lp , · · · , u m·lp }

The two parallel sequence embeddings are then fed into the Transformer backbone to autoregres-sively generate the target output within a decoder-only architecture. Here, we replace the conven-tional attention mechanism in decoder-only Transformers with a Time Bias Attention (Sec. 3.2) to correct attention biases caused by inter-patch structural discrepancies. During autoregressive gen-eration, we employ a hybrid causal mask (Sec. 3.3) to capture complex contextual dependencies in irregular multivariate time series. 3.1 TIME -A WARE PATCH ENCODING 

Irregular multivariate time series inputs typically consist of two parts: the observed time series val-ues and their corresponding timestamp. For the time series values, we follow the standard paradigm of mainstream TSFMs, transforming them into continuous patch tokens via patching and normaliza-tion. Regarding the timestamp, existing TSFMs commonly inject it using positional encoding (PE), which suffers from significant performance degradation when handling irregularly sampled data due to two main reasons: 1) PE is unable to represent the non-linear temporal positions between patches. 2) PE fails to characterize the irregular temporal structure within patches. To address these issues, we propose a Time-aware Patch Encoding module comprising continuous time mapping and intra-patch aggregation. This module constructs a continuous temporal structure representation that unifies the format for extracting temporal features from irregularly sampled patch-based data. 3.1.1 CONTINUOUS TIME MAPPING

3.4.1 DATASET DESCRIPTION 

To train foundation models tailored for IMTS, we collected and constructed the Versatile Time Series Archive dataset (VersaTSA). This dataset is designed to support IMTS pretraining by preserving the original sampling sparsity inherent in the data. VersaTSA comprises the following datasets: 1) Asyn-chronously sampled torque data from offshore wind power and wave height measurements, with a maximum sampling frequency of 50 Hz, collected over one week. 2) The LOTSA dataset (Woo et al., 2024), from which padding values have been removed to restore the original irregular sam-pling distribution. 3) The irregular multivariate time series portion of the Time-IMM dataset (Chang et al., 2025), where only the time series data are retained, excluding unstructured textual informa-tion. To facilitate efficient integration with deep learning pipelines, VersaTSA employs Apache Arrow (Richardson et al., 2025) for unified serialized storage. The resulting archive contains over 30 billion observations. Detailed dataset statistics and preprocessing metadata are provided in the project repository 1.3.4.2 HOLD -OUT PROTOCOL 

To prevent any data leakage in the downstream zero-shot evaluation, the three IMTS evaluation datasets used in Section 4.2.1 (PhysioNet, Human Activity, and USHCN) and the six regular MTS evaluation datasets used in Section 4.2.2 are strictly excluded from VersaTSA. We further filter out any sub-corpus from LOTSA whose source overlaps with these benchmarks. Pretraining and downstream evaluation therefore operate on disjoint data sources. 3.5 UNI TIMS T RAINING STRATEGY 

The pre-training process is conducted on the VersaTSA archive to equip the model with universal representations under irregular temporal fluctuation, aiming to: 1) capture complex dependencies in irregular samples, and 2) enable robust zero-shot forecasting via large-scale autoregressive train-ing. To achieve these goals, we implement a training strategy based on dynamic window partition-ing. Specifically, for each training sample extracted from the long-term sequences in VersaTSA, we randomly select a partition point followed by the Beta distribution (Johnson, 2016), splitting the patch sequence {p1, p 2, . . . , p m+n} into a historical prefix segment P and a target segment T .For example, given a partition point m sampled from a Beta distribution, P denotes the index set corresponding to the combined historical observations and covariates, i.e., Yt1:tm ∪ Z t1:tm+n . The target segment T includes the indices of the query set Q = {(t, c ) | t ∈ (tm, t m+n], c ∈ C Y }

and the special forecasting token [F] . During autoregressive generation, the prefix serves as the historical context where bidirectional information flow is permitted, while the target is forecasted causally, step-by-step. This structure is autoregressive because each target token conditions on the full prefix and only the previously generated target tokens, excluding future target information. 4.1.3 IMPLEMENT DETAILS 

We implement U NI TIMS in three configurations: Small, Base, and Large, with detailed specifica-tions provided in Table 2. All experiments are implemented using PyTorch (Paszke et al., 2019) and conducted on NVIDIA H100-NVL GPUs using TF32 precision. All models are trained with a global batch size of 1024 and with 8 attention heads. The Small model is trained for 25,000 steps, while the Base and Large models are trained for 250,000 steps. We utilize the AdamW optimizer with hyperparameters β1 = 0 .9, β2 = 0 .98 , weight decay of 0.1, and a peak learning rate of 1 × 10 −3.The learning rate schedule includes a linear warmup over the first 10,000 steps, followed by cosine decay. During pretraining, we partition data by time-series indices rather than constructing samples with sliding windows, and then randomly sample subsequences with a fixed window size of 3840 from each selected time series. The sampling positions are drawn from a Beta distribution with parameters (1 .6, 2.4) . During inference, segments that are too short to form a complete patch are padded, and padded values are ignored. 4.2 OVERALL PERFORMANCE

Instead of concatenating the vector representations of multiple times-tamps within a patch and projecting them via a linear layer, we employed the average of these vector representations to represent the temporal features of the patch. We observed a significant deterioration in normalized MAE when removing the Patch-aware time embedding. 5 RELATED WORK 

5.1 TIME SERIES FOUNDATION MODELS 

With the success of Large Language Models, the architecture of Foundation Models has seen a sig-nificant convergence towards the Transformer (Liang et al., 2024). Meanwhile, the patch-based approach has become a widespread practice in Time Series Foundation Models (TSFMs) after PatchTST (Nie et al., 2023) reduced the quadratic computational complexity by introducing a time series chunk to serve as the Transformer’s (Vaswani et al., 2023) input. Among these, Timer (Liu et al., 2024b), TimesFM (Das et al., 2024) and AutoHFormer (Zhang et al., 2025a) process multi-modal sequences via channel-independent methods, demonstrating strong zero-shot prediction ca-pabilities by pre-training. The latest Chronos-2 (Ansari et al., 2025) proposes Group Attention, which is synchronous in patch index and cross-variate to improve the model’s ability to understand the context of multivariate time series. Such TSFMs center on the paradigm of “universal forecast-ing”, like Chronos (Ansari et al., 2024), Moirai (Woo et al., 2024), TimeGPT (Garza et al., 2024), and Sundial (Liu et al., 2025), which have demonstrated impressive zero-shot or few-shot gener-alization across diverse downstream domains after a single pre-training phase (Bommasani et al., 2022). However, these approaches rely on either channel independence or synchronous channel dependence under the assumption of regular sampling grids, causing failures to capture the latent context of asynchronously sampled irregular multivariate time series. 5.2 IRREGULAR MULTIVARIATE TIME SERIES FORECASTING

A variety of methods, such as kernel method (Rehfeld et al., 2011), multiple imputation (White et al., 2011), and EM algorithm (Garc´ ıa-Laencina et al., 2010), have developed to impute these missing values for irregular time series. 6 CONCLUSION 

We introduce U NI TIMS, a time-aware decoder-only Transformer with a hybrid causal mask for irregular multivariate time series forecasting (IMTS), with a particular focus on informative miss-ingness and complex contextual dependencies issues in IMTS. We also introduced VersaTSA, a large collection of IMTS data for pretraining. Experiments on several datasets and benchmarks demonstrate U NI TIMS achieves superior zero-shot performance on IMTS and remains competi-tive on regular multivariate time series. Despite its strong out-of-the-box performance on IMTS, the inference efficiency of U NI TIMS remains an important practical consideration. Incorporating inference acceleration techniques like KV cache may significantly improve its efficiency and scala-bility. In addition, attention mechanisms similar to the hybrid causal mask have demonstrated strong capabilities in both natural language understanding and text generation in natural language process-ing. This suggests that their potential for time-series understanding and generation warrants further investigation. REPRODUCIBILITY STATEMENT
